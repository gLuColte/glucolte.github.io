ASG balances instance capacity; ELB balances requests. This is an uneven snapshot of 10 healthy, equal-weight targets. Assume 100 requests/s, split equally between two load balancer nodes, with uniform routing and no stickiness.

One AWS Region · ap-southeast-2 · one regional load balancer

100 requests/s → LB node A (50) + LB node B (50)

Each LB node can send its share to all 10 targets across both AZs.

Cross-zone ON: 100% ÷ 10 = 10% per target. AZ A receives 20%; AZ B receives 80%.

Why 2 + 8? ASG normally tries to balance instances across enabled AZs; this could be a temporary imbalance. Changing ELB cross-zone routing does not move instances or change the ASG desired capacity.

Defaults. ALB enables cross-zone at the load balancer; a target group can disable it. NLB disables it by default. Actual shares depend on routing, health, weights and connections; the percentages above are an educational model.

Across Regions? Route 53 routing policies or Global Accelerator → separate regional load balancers → separate regional ASGs. Cross-zone means AZs within one Region; one ASG does not span Regions.
