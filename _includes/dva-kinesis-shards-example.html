Records flow through shards

A record is one event: a data blob + partition key, assigned a sequence number when accepted. A shard is an ordered lane of records with its own throughput capacity. A stream contains those lanes. A record belongs to one shard; the shard is not a batch sent to the consumer.

300 records/s exceed one shard's sustained byte budget. Some writes can be throttled; retry failed records with backoff and jitter.

Per shard: min(1,000 records/s, 1,024 KiB/s ÷ 4 KiB) = 256 records/s. At 300 records/s, at least 2 evenly loaded shards are needed before headroom.

Two limits, one lane. Each shard supports sustained writes up to 1 MiB/s and 1,000 records/s; whichever is reached first limits you. At 4 KiB, bytes win. With 512-byte records, the record-count limit would win. These are rates, not the shard's total storage size.

Reading is independent. Shared-throughput consumers share up to 2 MiB/s per shard; enhanced fan-out provides up to 2 MiB/s per shard per registered consumer. Reading does not delete records. Retention controls replay; there is no global order across shards. PutRecords retries can also change producer event order.

Capacity ≠ retention. The lane continues accepting records over time; it does not fill at 1 MiB and move to the next shard. Control-plane scaling changes the shard layout; data-plane calls write/read records. On-demand manages capacity for you, but a hot partition key still needs attention.
